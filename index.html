1The case for changeFive compounding facts, AI named directly, and Arizona demand evidence.UPDATEDBob outline §1 · Tim's note · AZ research

High school is not working academically, developmentally, or as a bridge to adulthood. Five facts compound: twelfth-grade NAEP scores at record lows while graduation rates hit record highs, so the diploma certifies little beyond attendance; high school, college, and work are poorly aligned; engagement falls every year from 5th grade through 12th; youth distress is at the highest recorded levels; public confidence in schools has fallen from roughly 60% in the mid-1970s to the low 30s. Alternatives (microschools, ESAs, vouchers) grow without evidence or accountability because families sense what the data confirms. This is an institution-level problem; no single fix addresses it. The argument is grounded in the structural failures of the secondary system rather than the promise of AI, though AI is now named directly: Caitrin and Diane agree it is the elephant in the room, with the potential to be the biggest equalizer and the biggest destroyer of equity in education.

Added Sept 24: the demand is no longer inferred. In Arizona, 55% of voters want high school fundamentally redesigned rather than improved; when Phoenix focus-group families were asked to design teenage education from scratch, they described this model's core elements before any concept was shown to them; and not one participant, across income levels, chose traditional public high school as their first choice. The named enrollment barrier is fear of lock-in; the strongest trust builder is employer partnerships with placement commitments.

Sources: Bob's outline §1 ↗ · Tim's note ↗ · polling review ↗

2The North StarThe settled statement, what it means, and what it does not mean.settled Aug 6 · final language Aug 17
Every young person develops a working hypothesis of the life they want and builds a credible pathway to it. They are deeply engaged in learning that equips them to live a dignified and fulfilled life. The system exists to serve their becoming. The young person is at the center.

What it means and does not mean: it dissolves the college-versus-career binary (academic mastery and applied, real-stakes learning are simultaneous requirements for every student); the working hypothesis is provisional and revisable, never premature tracking; engagement is a daily design criterion; a Portrait of a Graduate is insufficient unless the school experience itself changes; student agency does not mean abandoning a common knowledge base. Historically this is a third way between the whole-human camp and the workforce camp, the fight every reform era has refought.

Sources: Diane's Precision Core v.2 ↗ · Tim's note ↗ · Diane has asked for a structured conversation on purpose alignment; not yet held

3The theory of changeShadow system, infection, sustained public demand. Now written, in Diane's expanded paper.UPDATEDDiane's expanded paper, Sept 22

Change of this scale happens in three parts: build a shadow system that is a full representation of the new system, core plus models; infect the existing system with the change; create sustained public demand for the new thing. Technology is the transfer agent: it is how the shadow system runs, how learning accumulates across sites, and how the change spreads. Public demand is a funded workstream with demand research, storytelling, and versions of the story that carry in rural and blue-collar communities.

4The school modelOutcomes → Experiences → Structures → Infrastructure; engagement as the daily test; the non-negotiables now contested.2 TENSIONSUPDATEDAylon's logic · Caitrin's POV · Diane's comments

Organizing logic: Outcomes → Experiences → Structures → Infrastructure. Outcomes name who graduates become (working hypothesis, pathway, portrait). Experiences name what students live every day. Structures name the design decisions that produce those experiences. Infrastructure (section 5) names what the structures stand on. This resolves the vocabulary collision in the comments: Diane's Precision Core sits at the infrastructure level, Bob's instructional core sits at the structures level, and both are right. Experiences and structures stay separate levels because one experience is produced by many design decisions at once; collapsing them loses the central design question, whether the structure we built is producing the experience we want.

The experience layer. Engagement is the daily test, defined as intrinsic and low-anxiety, and measured directly. The Leaps ↗ are a ready candidate instrument (agency, connection and community, high expectations with rigorous learning, relevance, customization, whole-child focus), though any good measure of the daily experience does the job. Field evidence says these experiences stack; schools that combine several consistently outperform schools that lean on one. Caitrin's POV (Sept 22) arrives at this layer from motivation science: precision learning and self-direction reinforce one another, and autonomy is simultaneously a condition students experience, a capacity they practice, and an outcome the model is accountable for.

Structures: the shared baseline. Participating schools begin from a specified model. Non-negotiables: strong culture, belonging, and relationships; mastery-based progression; retrieval and consolidation practice; high expectations plus support; a strong common knowledge core; reliable assessment; guided personalization. Core practices: flexible mastery-based grouping; strong advisory; shared high-quality curriculum; real-world and applied learning with stakes; portfolio and performance assessment; explicit instruction for novices, deeper learning as expertise develops. Room for variation: campus-based versus distributed, time allocation between academics and applied learning, pace of redesign.

Caitrin's POV now argues the developmental ramp in writing: "effective scaffolding should contain a theory of its own disappearance," with responsibility transferring as capability grows. Held for now: guided personalization for novices, autonomy expanding as demonstrated mastery grows, which two of three written positions now support. Sept 29.

5The shared infrastructure: the Precision CoreThe industrial core replaced element by element; the platform as orchestrator rather than tutor.2 TENSIONSUPDATEDDiane's frame · Bob's spec · Caitrin's AI-infrastructure hypothesis

The industrial core is encoded in policy and finance, case law, databases, and culture, which is why local design alone cannot escape it. Its replacement, the Precision Core, re-founds each element: the diploma becomes a validated working hypothesis and pathway, with graduation as a waypoint; the transcript becomes a validated narrative and portfolio of what a young person knows and can do; grade levels invert (learning fixed, time variable); courses give way to interdisciplinary, competency-organized learning; the master schedule, seat time, and scope-and-sequence yield to schedules built around learning; evaluation moves to valid, unconflicted methods including technology; assessment becomes on-demand and ambient; accountability moves to real-world outcomes; money follows the young person.

The platform. One mastery-native system where competencies replace credits as the primary data object, built as one system because legacy SIS and LMS databases encode the industrial core and cannot be retrofitted. It carries: competency and mastery tracking; each student's purpose and pathway; portfolios and authentic evidence; visibility into student thinking and process; adaptive scheduling; guardrailed AI tutoring (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025: unguarded AI lifted practice performance 48% and cut unaided exam performance 17%; guardrails preserved gains); dashboards for students, advisors, families, and leaders; wellbeing signals; real-world experience management; portable learner records. Throughout: AI carries the complexity, adults keep the relationship.

From tutor to infrastructure (added Sept 24). Caitrin's POV sharpens the platform's job with new evidence: in a two-year randomized study, students used an AI tutor on only a third of practice days and asked it for help after a mistake 17% of the time, with modest gains; AI's value showed up when embedded in a structured mastery workflow that turned mistakes into productive learning moments. The platform is therefore an orchestrator: interpreting student work, diagnosing why a student is stuck, deciding what happens next, and surfacing the right action to adults, with the tutor as one component. Diane's assessment language joins the spec (ambient, legible, and usable by the student, designed for student progress and clarity rather than system accountability), as do her criteria for the core technologists: they build with schools rather than for them, run hybrid waterfall-agile, and hold consumer-grade experience alongside SaaS-grade security and integration.

6The design and implementation engineWho is on the ground every day: design partners, the five conditions, kits, a change platform as a public good.UPDATEDAylon, Sept 3 · corroborated by Caitrin's POV

The group is clear that the technology gets co-built with the schools, side by side. The same is not yet settled for the change work itself: who is on the ground with these schools every day, and how does a school get from signing up to running the model? Every effort in the history brief failed partly here. The NASSP Model Schools Project (the Trump plan, 1969–1977) had no shared design and implementation capacity: "the book was the integration layer, which is to say there wasn't one," and each school was its own general contractor. New Hampshire and Indiana produced permission nobody knew how to use. This section proposes a second infrastructure, co-built with the schools the way the platform is: infrastructure for the change process itself, as a public good.

Components: embedded design partners with each school; a codified design journey methodology built on community-based design, with students, families, educators, and community members as co-designers; the five conditions that predict success (conviction, clarity, capacity, coalition, culture) used as an admissions screen and an ongoing diagnostic, conditions-first; packaged design kits so no school starts from scratch; an AI-enabled change platform that codifies what each journey learns so every subsequent school starts further ahead. Talent lives here too: hiring, onboarding, and developing adults for redesigned roles, and the coaching capacity the field does not yet have.

Corroboration (Sept 24). This section no longer rests on one voice. Caitrin's POV names shared design infrastructure among the things the whole thesis is testing ("what kinds of shared infrastructure can help school design move faster, with greater quality and coherence, than would be possible school by school"), and her comment on the outline flags that the infrastructure section under-covers exactly this design and iteration capacity.

7Measurement, evidence, and the credentialExperience data as leading indicators; an academic floor; the Carnegie Diploma; the moment students want to stop.2 TENSIONSUPDATEDthree legs · Caitrin's POV · AZ poll

First, the experience layer is the measurement spine. Experience and engagement instruments run from month one, giving leading indicators (agency, belonging, relevance, high expectations with support) years before outcome data matures. The stacking evidence carries the funder-floor argument: KIPP schools pairing rigor with community outperform rigor-only KIPP schools; the strongest Rocketship and Summit implementations stacked rigor, community, agency, and customization. The experiences are the most direct lever to the outcomes, and the claim has evidence behind it. Second, an academic floor is collected from day one, answering first-do-no-harm without letting the old system's measures define the new one. Third, the credential. The Carnegie Diploma is part of the core build: assembled from authentic work wherever learning happens, made feasible by multimodal AI assessment, with comparative-judgment methods (Christodoulou / No More Marking) under active diligence for the assessment engine.

Added Sept 24. Caitrin's POV contributes the sharpest diagnostic in the measurement conversation: the moment that matters is what a student does immediately after difficulty, error, boredom, or frustration; persistence, help-seeking, and recovery say more than platform usage. And the Arizona poll strengthens the authentic-assessment leg from the public's side: 91% of voters worry students will pass classes without learning because of AI, and hands-on projects with in-person proof of learning restore confidence for 89%.

TENSION AI as validator. Whether AI may be the sole validator of complex or high-stakes learning is undecided; NYC policy reads as a red light on AI grading, amber on diagnostics. Held for now: AI-assisted evidence synthesis, human validation on consequential decisions. Sept 29.
TENSION The diploma's equity critique. Bob's outline raises validity and social-capital concerns about portfolio-heavy credentialing. Tim's paper argues the credential is an equalizer and does not address the critique; it is new material for him to respond to. Sept 29.

8The pilotThree years, fully funded upfront, permissionless environments; two written proposals on size; Arizona leading on where.2 TENSIONSUPDATEDBob's outline vs. Diane's alternative · AZ research

Shape held for now: a three-year project, fully funded upfront, on Diane's sequence. Year 0 raises everything. Year 1 is disciplined planning: total alignment, core technology usable, school models designed, operations readied. Year 2 launches the first schools with rapid improvement cycles. Year 3 launches the second cohort and decides whether there is a second project. Operators sign a full sheet of paper: the North Star, a blueprint they shape and express in context, and the shared platform as the operating system, required. Environments are permissionless: new starts, private and ESA-funded schools, or state-waivered public schools, exempt from industrial-core requirements. The cohort spans expressions of the model and must include schools serving students who arrive below grade level alongside middle-class families; the evidence is not credible otherwise. Every student, family, and educator opts in with full understanding, and the project builds off-ramps in case schools do not continue past the project's life.

TENSION Size of the bet NOW TWO WRITTEN PROPOSALS. Bob's outline: $50M / 4 years / 12–15 schools, "roughly one-third the capital for a longer runway." Diane (Sept 21) inserted her three-year Precision Core project into the outline doc as a written alternative: Year 0 raises everything, Year 1 plans and builds, Years 2 and 3 launch cohorts, schools exempt from all industrial-core requirements, every participant opting in with off-ramps. Held for now: the three-year fully-funded shape; cohort size open pending a budget build. First item Sept 29.
TENSION New starts vs. transformations; and where AZ EVIDENCE ADDED. The outline's language leans new schools; Bob's systems memo weights both equally; Aylon holds both with differentiated expectations and timelines. On where: Arizona has emerged as the lead candidate for the permissionless environment, with a universal ESA serving 100K+ students, roughly $8K following each student, and documented public demand (55% want fundamental redesign). Stand Together is already organizing a five-year Arizona plan, which raises its own question: partner, parallel effort, or competitor. Held for now: both school types, differentiated; state question open with AZ leading. Sept 29.

9Governance and sustainabilityA small board, a lead partner with no organization behind them, a time-limited entity.1 NOTEtraces to Aug 6 · ratify in writing

Enough coordinating capacity to make a coherent system possible, without another permanent intermediary: a small fiduciary and executive board (5–9 seats); a participating-organizations council with influence but not fiduciary control; a full-time lead partner and consensus builder with no organization behind them; flexible contracting for technical, evaluation, and design expertise; a buyer and field-scanning capability; a deliberately time-limited entity winding down in 3.5–5 years; schools that sustain on public funding rather than permanent philanthropy. The proposal must answer Caitrin's questions directly: how members raising money for their own organizations explain the relationship, especially with overlapping donors; IP and upside sharing with technology partners; financial claw-backs when schools underdeliver.

What separates Skye from fifty years of reform
